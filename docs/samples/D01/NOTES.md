# D01 samples: notes

The baseline before any depth work: the curious explorer's first three hours on seeds 1, 42 and 9001, with the example text. Regenerate with `scraped-lang samples D01`.

**What reads well**
- Landmarks are told apart now: "the flat mountain", "the walled town", "the crossroads town". Each name comes from what the place is (its shape, walls, roads, setting), and stays the same from every side.
- Close looking pays a little: examining a stele says it bears writing, and examining an item shows its state.

**What still reads flat**
- **Walls of text.** Every look and arrival lists the weather, the ground, every building in town (up to thirty), the horizon and the season. A look has 88 words and about 29 facts at the median. D02's attention budget is for this.
- **Sameness.** Towns are the same handful of temples, houses, tombs and storehouses. Rooms read as "a hall, dim and close". Most things have nothing to them beyond their name.
- **Writing.** Reading is long lists of glyph descriptions, page after page, and most texts are tombs. That's right for decipherment, but nothing else on the stone or in the room gives the player a reason to care.
- **The world tells instead of showing.** "It is spring", "The land is parched", "The air is colder than it should be". These are the statements "Say little; let the player dig" asks to replace with evidence.
- **No senses.** No listening, smelling or touching: a room is only what's in it.
