# D08 — The magic, deepened

**Goal:** make writing as rich as the language that carries it. Many concepts have powers; spells can be aimed, qualified and conditioned; the old civilisation used writing as everyday technology; and the world is saturated with old spells, most small, which make it strange everywhere long before the player knows why.

**Depends on:** D07. **Design gates:** how common old magic is; whether writing was the old civilisation's technology (proposed defaults in `docs/DEPTH.md`).

**Done when:** the D08 targets are met, a world's live spells produce a wide range of strange but explorable places, and a player who understands the grammar can compose spells that do precise, varied things.

## Scope

### 1. Powers of concepts

Replace the three verbs (open, burn, break) with a **concept-to-power table** as data, covering the D06 lexicon:

- **Properties the simulation already has, extended:** heat and cold, light and dark, wet and dry, flow and stillness, open and closed, stable and crumbling, sound and silence.
- **New properties:** growth and decay (plants, D05), animals' behaviour (drawn, repelled, calmed, asleep), weight and lightness, visibility (hidden, revealed), strength (bound, held, loosed), direction (water uphill, wind), keeping (food unspoiled, things preserved).
- Every concept's power derives from its meaning (water: flow; seal: binding; eye: revealing), so a player can predict a word's power from what it means. The table is generated per culture from fixed principles, so it differs between worlds but stays logical.

### 2. The shape of a spell

A spell is a meaning in the potent register (M02 and D06's optative mood):

- **Target:** what it acts on: this door, every door here, the river, the named place, whoever enters, a kind of animal.
- **Predicate:** the change, from the power table.
- **Modifiers:** degree (a little, greatly), extent (here, to the hill, the whole valley), duration (until sunrise, until the moon is full, always).
- **Conditions:** "when someone enters", "if the water rises", "when the gate is shut". Conditional spells lie dormant until triggered, which gives traps, alarms, mechanisms and puzzles.
- **Negation and exceptions:** "let no one but the priest pass".
- Grammar errors misfire predictably (M09's model, extended to the new constructions).

### 3. Writing as technology

The old civilisation used everyday spells: a larder charm kept food cold, lamps that lit at dusk, a mill that turned with no water, a door that opened only for a household, a well that never ran dry, a field kept fertile, a bridge held up beyond its strength. They are generated by history alongside buildings (D03), objects (D04) and institutions (D07).

Many are still live, broken or half-working: a granary still cold after centuries, a street of lamps that still light at dusk, a fountain still running in a dead town, a door that still won't open for a stranger. That ruined infrastructure is most of the world's strangeness, and it reads first as a mystery of the place, not as magic.

### 4. Saturation and layering

- **Density:** every settlement has several small live spells; the land between has some; each region has at least one large one (the great inscriptions of M10).
- **Layering:** spells over spells, scraped and rewritten through history, so surface stacks (M08) are deep and meaningful: a wall holding a ward, written over a curse, written over a dedication.
- **Interaction:** spells combine through the simulation (a cold spell and a flow spell on the same river make a frozen fall), with conflicts resolved as M08 decided.
- Natural phenomena (D05) and spell phenomena are both common, so the player must learn to tell them apart.

### 5. Effects through every system

Spells act through places (D03), objects and machines (D04), ecology, weather and sky (D05), and the regional simulation (M10). Effects are perceived through the D02 attention model: evidence, never statements of cause.

## Content slots introduced

Cues for each new property's effects (as variables on shared families), dormant and triggered spells, misfires of new constructions. No slot may name a spell's meaning.

## Tests

- Every concept's power follows the table's principles; snapshot the table for three seeds.
- Every spell's effect is deterministic, perceptible through some cue, and never named.
- Conditional spells trigger exactly when their condition holds.
- Interactions are stable (no feedback loops that blow up).
- Fairness: every power a late-game goal needs is attested in the world with a visible effect.
- Performance: hundreds of live spells evaluate fast enough in WebAssembly.
