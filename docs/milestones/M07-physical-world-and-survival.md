# M07 — Physical world and survival

**Goal:** give the world reasons to be changed. Places have physical properties that interact, ordinary mechanisms can be worked, and the player has to stay warm, fed, watered and uninjured. Death ends the run.

**Depends on:** M06.

**Done when:** Jb can play several in-game days surviving with ordinary means (shelter, fire, water, food, routes), solve an obstacle by ordinary interaction (open a sluice so the ford drops), and die in at least two different ways.

## Design decisions

| Decision | Proposed default |
|---|---|
| Needs | Warmth, water, food, rest and injury. Coarse states, not meters ("chilled", "thirsty") |
| Pace | Needs change over hours, not minutes. A careful player rarely starves; a careless one freezes |
| Threats | Environmental (cold, drowning, falls, collapse) plus a small set of creatures with simple behaviour. No combat system; avoidance, hiding, deterrence (fire, noise) |
| Crafting | Minimal: fire, torch, simple shelter, water container. No crafting trees |
| Death | Permanent. The run ends and the end-of-run flow (M11 stub for now) follows |

## Scope

### In

1. **`sim` crate: local properties.** Per area and per room: temperature, light, wetness, water level and flow, airflow, sound, structural stability, vegetation. Sources (sun, fire, rivers, wind, decay) and sinks.
2. **Interactions** as data-driven rules:
   - cold freezes still water, ice bears weight above a thickness;
   - heat dries and can ignite dry material; wet wood resists fire;
   - flow erodes, floods low rooms, turns wheels;
   - darkness hides features; noise disturbs creatures and dislodges unstable stone;
   - instability leads to collapse, which blocks routes and opens others.
   - Each rule is an entry in a table that M08 effects can also trigger.
3. **Mechanisms:** doors, bars and locks, sluices and gates, levers, wheels, bridges (raise/lower), lamps and braziers, wells. Their states change properties.
4. **Items:** carryable objects with properties (light source, container, fuel, tool, food). Weight limit. Tools with ordinary uses (a pry bar opens stuck doors). The three writing tools are just items here; they get their powers in M08–M09.
5. **Needs and body:** warmth from temperature, clothing, wetness, shelter and fire; water and food sources (rivers, wells, foraging by biome, stores in sites); rest and fatigue; injuries from falls, collapse and creatures, and healing over time.
6. **Threats:** creatures generated by biome and history (a few archetypes: scavengers, territorial grazers, ambush predators, something stranger in deep places). Simple state machines: wander, notice, approach, flee. They interrupt travel only if they could not be seen beforehand (M06 rule).
7. **Death:** causes are recorded with detail (where, why, what the player was doing) for the end-of-run summary.
8. **Obstacles:** M04 structures and terrain gain obstacles that ordinary means can solve, so pre-writing play has real goals: flooded passages, deep rivers, sealed doors, dark stairs, cold uplands.

### Out

Writing-driven effects (M08), slow regional change (M10).

## Content slots introduced

Property cues in descriptions (cold, damp, draughty, dark, unstable); mechanism states and operation; item examine and use; need states and their transitions ("You are shivering"); injury and recovery; creature sightings, approach, flight; death narration per cause; obstacle descriptions.

## Tests

- Rule table unit tests (each interaction).
- Conservation sanity (water doesn't appear from nowhere; fire needs fuel).
- Need balance: a scripted "careful survivor" bot lasts at least N days on most seeds; a "reckless" bot dies within a range. This keeps difficulty in bounds.
- Creatures never ambush from visible positions.
- Every generated obstacle on the main routes has at least one ordinary solution or an alternative route (writing can be a better solution, but not the only one early on).
