# D11 — Problems only writing solves

**Goal:** reward the players who do realise. The late game holds places, puzzles and destinations that need real fluency in the language and the layering rules, with the great inscriptions and the deepest text as its summits.

**Depends on:** D10.

**Done when:** each world has a late game that a fluent player can work through with understanding rather than trial and error, and that a non-fluent player can see but not enter.

## Scope

### 1. Places writing opens

Places sealed or shaped by old spells that only writing can undo or work with: a valley held in winter, a drowned district, a door that opens only for a named household, a library hidden from sight, a bridge that holds only while a spell lasts. Each is visible and intriguing well before it can be entered.

### 2. Puzzles of composition

- Problems whose solution is a spell the player must compose, using grammar they've learned: the right target, modifier and condition (D09), written over a surface whose lower layers constrain what fits (M09).
- **Chains:** solving one spell reveals the next (scraping a ward exposes the layer beneath, which explains the next lock).
- **Deep reading** matters: what lies two or three layers down determines what can be written on top.
- Multiple solutions where the language allows, so understanding beats memorising.

### 3. The great inscriptions as destinations

The great inscriptions (M10) become journeys: reaching each needs survival, exploration and writing; understanding each needs the history (D08); changing each has regional consequences and trade-offs (M10).

### 4. The deepest text

The root event and deepest text (M11) are the end of a long thread through the whole world's history, with the departure-by-writing (M11) as one of its rewards.

## Tests

- Each writing-gated place is visible and reachable to its door without writing.
- Each composition puzzle is solvable by the scholar bot from attested grammar and vocabulary alone.
- Chains are acyclic and solvable in more than one order where intended.
- Fairness: every late-game goal's evidence is attested.
